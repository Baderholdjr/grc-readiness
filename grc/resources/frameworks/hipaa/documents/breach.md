# {{title}}

## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}} discovers, assesses and documents possible breaches of unsecured PHI. The notices that follow depend on {{client}}'s role and are in the covered entity or business associate procedure.

## Discovery

A breach is treated as discovered on the first day it is known to {{client}}, or would have been known with reasonable diligence, to anyone in its workforce or any agent other than the person who committed it. Every notice deadline runs from that date, so it is recorded for each incident.

## Assessment

An acquisition, access, use or disclosure of PHI not permitted by the Privacy Rule is presumed a breach. It is not a breach if one of the three exceptions applies:

- an unintentional, good-faith acquisition, access or use by a workforce member or person acting under {{client}}'s authority, within the scope of that authority, with no further impermissible use or disclosure;
- an inadvertent disclosure between people authorized to access PHI at the same covered entity, business associate or organized health care arrangement, with no further impermissible use or disclosure;
- a disclosure where there is a good-faith belief that the recipient could not reasonably have retained the information.

Otherwise it is a breach unless a documented assessment of at least these four factors shows a low probability that the PHI has been compromised: the nature and extent of the PHI involved, the unauthorized person who used it or received it, whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed, and the extent to which the risk has been mitigated.

Notification duties apply only to unsecured PHI. PHI encrypted or destroyed in line with HHS guidance on rendering PHI unusable, unreadable or indecipherable is secured, and its loss is not a reportable breach.

## Law enforcement delay

If a law enforcement official states that a notice or posting would impede a criminal investigation or damage national security:

- for a written statement that names a time period, the notice is delayed for that period;
- for an oral statement, the statement and the official's identity are documented and the notice is delayed no longer than 30 days from the oral statement, unless a written statement is provided in that time.

## Administrative requirements

The Privacy Rule's administrative requirements apply to breach handling: workforce training on these procedures, a way to complain, sanctions for workforce members who don't follow them, no retaliation and no waiver of rights, and keeping these policies and procedures up to date (164.530(b), (d), (e), (g), (h), (i) and (j)).

## Records

Discovery dates, assessments, exception determinations, notices and the reasons for any delay are kept for six years. {{client}} carries the burden of proof that every required notice was made, or that a use or disclosure was not a breach.
