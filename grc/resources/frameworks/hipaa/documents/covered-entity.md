# {{title}}

## Purpose

This procedure sets the duties {{client}} has as a covered entity: obtaining assurances from business associates and giving the notices the Breach Notification Rule requires after a breach of unsecured PHI.

## Business associate assurances

{{client}} lets a business associate create, receive, maintain or transmit ePHI on its behalf only after a business associate agreement gives satisfactory written assurances that it will safeguard the information.

## Notifying individuals

Each affected individual is notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery, by first-class mail (or email, if the individual agreed to it). The next of kin or personal representative of a deceased individual is notified by mail if the address is known. Where there may be imminent misuse, {{client}} may also call the individual.

## Content of the notice

Each notice is written in plain language and includes:

- a brief description of what happened, with the date of the breach and the date of discovery, if known;
- the types of unsecured PHI involved (for example name, Social Security number, date of birth, diagnosis);
- the steps individuals should take to protect themselves;
- what {{client}} is doing to investigate, mitigate harm and protect against further breaches;
- contact procedures, including a toll-free number, an email address, a website or a postal address.

## Substitute notice

When contact information is out of date or insufficient:

- for fewer than 10 individuals, notice is given by an alternative written notice, telephone or other means;
- for 10 or more, notice is either a conspicuous posting on {{client}}'s home page for 90 days or a conspicuous notice in major print or broadcast media where the individuals likely live, with a toll-free number that stays active for at least 90 days.

## Notifying the media

When a breach involves more than 500 residents of a State or jurisdiction, prominent media outlets serving it are notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery, with the same content as the individual notice.

## Notifying HHS

- 500 or more individuals: HHS is notified at the same time as the individuals, through the HHS breach portal.
- Fewer than 500: the breach is entered in the breach log, and the log is reported to HHS no later than 60 days after the end of each calendar year.
