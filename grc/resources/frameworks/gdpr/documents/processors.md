## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}}, as a controller, chooses, contracts with and monitors its processors (Art. 28), and how it shares responsibilities with joint controllers (Art. 26).

## Choosing processors

Before a vendor processes personal data for {{client}}, the vendor owner and [[TODO: the privacy or security lead]] check that it gives sufficient guarantees of appropriate technical and organisational measures (Art. 28(1)). The check looks at security certifications or audit reports, sub-processors, data locations and transfers, and breach history. ## Processor contracts

No processor receives personal data before a written contract is signed (Art. 28(9) allows electronic form). The contract sets out the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the types of personal data, the categories of people, and {{client}}'s rights and obligations. It requires the processor to:

- (a) process only on {{client}}'s documented instructions, including on transfers, and say if an instruction infringes the law;
- (b) bind its staff to confidentiality;
- (c) take the security measures required by Art. 32;
- (d) use sub-processors only with {{client}}'s authorisation and on the same terms;
- (e) help {{client}} answer rights requests;
- (f) help {{client}} with security, breach notification, DPIAs and prior consultation;
- (g) delete or return all personal data at the end of the service, at {{client}}'s choice;
- (h) provide the information needed to show compliance and allow audits.

The contract also requires the processor to notify {{client}} of a personal data breach without undue delay, within [[TODO: contracted hours]].

## Monitoring processors

The vendor owner reviews each processor at least [[TODO: frequency]], and more often for high-risk processing. The review checks updated audit reports, sub-processor changes, which {{client}} may object to, incidents and transfers. Problems are tracked to closure, and the processor is replaced if they are not fixed.

## Joint controllers

Where {{client}} and another organisation jointly decide the purposes and means of processing, they sign an arrangement setting out who does what, in particular for privacy notices and rights requests, and may name a contact point (Art. 26(1)). The essence of the arrangement is made available to the people concerned. People can still exercise their rights against either controller (Art. 26(3)).
