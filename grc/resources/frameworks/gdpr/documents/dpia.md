## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}} assesses processing likely to result in a high risk to people before it starts (Art. 35), and when it consults the supervisory authority first (Art. 36).

## Screening

Every new or changed processing activity is screened during the privacy review, before it starts. A DPIA is required where processing is likely to result in a high risk, in particular for new technologies, and always for (Art. 35(3)):

- a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects, including profiling, on which decisions with legal or similarly significant effects are based;
- large-scale processing of special category data or criminal offence data;
- systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale.

The screening also checks the lists of processing operations published by the supervisory authorities concerned (Art. 35(4)), starting with [[TODO: the lead or main supervisory authority]], and the EDPB criteria, such as vulnerable people, innovative technology, matching datasets or large scale. As a rule of thumb, where two or more criteria apply a DPIA is done; one criterion can be enough. The screening result and its reasons are recorded, even when no DPIA is needed.

## Carrying out a DPIA

The process owner leads the DPIA, and {{client}} seeks the advice of the DPO, where one is designated (Art. 35(2)). The DPIA contains at least (Art. 35(7)):

- a systematic description of the processing and its purposes, including any legitimate interest;
- an assessment of its necessity and proportionality;
- an assessment of the risks to people's rights and freedoms;
- the measures to address those risks, including safeguards and security, and how compliance is shown.

Where appropriate, the views of the people affected or their representatives are sought (Art. 35(9)). An accountable manager signs off the residual risk. The DPIA is kept at [[TODO: location]] and reviewed when the risk changes (Art. 35(11)).

## Prior consultation

If the DPIA shows the processing would result in a high risk that {{client}}'s measures cannot reduce, {{client}} consults the supervisory authority before starting (Art. 36(1)). It provides the DPIA, the responsibilities of the controllers and processors involved, the purposes and means, the measures and safeguards, and the DPO's contact details (Art. 36(3)). The authority gives written advice within eight weeks, extendable by six weeks for complex processing. Processing does not start in the meantime.
