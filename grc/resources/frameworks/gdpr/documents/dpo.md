## Purpose

This charter sets why {{client}} has a data protection officer (DPO), and the DPO's position and tasks (Art. 37 to Art. 39).

## Designation

A DPO must be designated where (Art. 37(1)):

- processing is carried out by a public authority or body, except courts acting in their judicial capacity;
- the core activities consist of processing that requires regular and systematic monitoring of people on a large scale;
- the core activities consist of large-scale processing of special category data or criminal offence data.

The DPO is chosen for expert knowledge of data protection law and practice and may be a staff member or work under a service contract (Art. 37(5)-(6)).

## Independence and resources

- The DPO is involved properly and in good time in all data protection issues (Art. 38(1)).
- {{client}} gives the DPO the resources, access to personal data and processing operations, and training needed (Art. 38(2)).
- The DPO receives no instructions about how to carry out their tasks, is not dismissed or penalised for performing them, and reports directly to the highest management level, [[TODO: e.g. the board or CEO]] (Art. 38(3)).
- People may contact the DPO about any issue on their data and rights (Art. 38(4)). The DPO keeps the matters confidential (Art. 38(5)).
- The DPO may hold other tasks only if they cause no conflict of interests. Roles that decide purposes and means of processing, such as [[TODO: roles judged conflicting]], are not combined with the DPO role. The conflict check is documented (Art. 38(6)).

## Tasks

The DPO, with due regard to the risk of processing (Art. 39):

- informs and advises {{client}} and its staff about their data protection obligations;
- monitors compliance, including assigning responsibilities, awareness-raising, training and audits;
- advises on DPIAs and monitors their performance;
- cooperates with the supervisory authority and is its contact point, including for prior consultation.
