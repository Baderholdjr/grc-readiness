## Purpose

Each notice has an owner, a version and an effective date. The notice is given at the time the data is collected.

## What every notice contains

- {{client}}'s identity and contact details, and those of its EU representative and DPO where it has them;
- the purposes and the lawful basis for each, and the legitimate interests relied on;
- the recipients or categories of recipients;
- any transfer outside the EEA, the adequacy decision or safeguard used, and how to get a copy;
- how long data is kept, or the criteria used to decide;
- the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability, and the right to withdraw consent at any time;
- the right to complain to a supervisory authority;
- whether providing the data is a legal or contractual requirement, and what happens if it is not provided;
- any automated decision-making, including profiling, under Art. 22, with meaningful information about the logic and its likely consequences.

The right to object is shown clearly and separately from other information, at the latest at the first communication (Art. 21(4)). Before data is used for a new purpose, people are told about it first (Art. 13(3)).

## Data from other sources

When data is not collected from the person, the notice also says what categories of data are held and where they came from, including whether from public sources (Art. 14). It is given within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month, or at first contact, or before the data is first disclosed to another recipient, whichever is earliest (Art. 14(3)).

An Art. 14(5) exception (the person already has the information, notice is impossible or would take disproportionate effort, the law expressly provides for obtaining the data, or professional secrecy) is used only after a documented assessment. Where disproportionate effort is relied on, {{client}} still protects the person, including by making the information publicly available.
