## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}} keeps a written record of the processing activities it carries out as a controller (Art. 30(1)).

## What the record contains

The record is kept in writing, which includes electronic form, in [[TODO: tool or location]]. For each processing activity it lists:

- the name and contact details of {{client}}, any joint controller, its EU representative and DPO, where applicable;
- the purposes of the processing;
- the categories of people and the categories of personal data;
- the categories of recipients, including those in third countries or international organisations;
- transfers to a third country or international organisation, which country, and, for transfers under the second subparagraph of Art. 49(1), the documented safeguards;
- where possible, the time limits for erasure of each category of data;
- where possible, a general description of the security measures under Art. 32(1).

{{client}} also records the lawful basis, any Art. 9 or Art. 10 condition, the systems used, the processors involved and whether a DPIA was done. These help show compliance and keep the privacy notices accurate.

The Art. 30(5) exception for organisations with fewer than 250 employees does not apply when the processing is likely to result in a risk to people's rights, is not occasional, or includes special category or criminal offence data. Regular processing such as payroll or customer records is not occasional, so {{client}} keeps the record in any case.

## Keeping it current

- Each activity has an owner who updates the record before a change to its purpose, data, recipients, systems, processors or transfers goes live.
- The privacy review in the development lifecycle and new processor onboarding both check the record.
