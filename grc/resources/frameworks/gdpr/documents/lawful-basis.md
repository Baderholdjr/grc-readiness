# {{title}}

## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}} chooses and records a lawful basis for each processing purpose (Art. 6), and how it collects and manages consent (Art. 7).

## Choosing a lawful basis

Before processing starts, the process owner picks one basis per purpose from Art. 6(1) and records it in the records of processing:

- (a) consent;
- (b) necessary for a contract with the person, or steps at their request before one;
- (c) necessary for a legal obligation on {{client}};
- (d) necessary to protect someone's vital interests;
- (e) necessary for a task in the public interest or official authority;
- (f) necessary for {{client}}'s or a third party's legitimate interests, unless overridden by the person's interests or rights.

"Necessary" means the purpose cannot reasonably be achieved with less data or a less intrusive method. The basis is not switched later to keep processing going. Using data for a new purpose needs a compatibility check under Art. 6(4) or a new basis. Special category and criminal offence data also need a condition from the Special Category and Criminal Offence Data Procedure.

## Legitimate interests assessments

Where (f) is used, a legitimate interests assessment is completed and kept with the record. It covers three questions:

1. Is the interest legitimate and clearly stated?
2. Is the processing necessary for it?
3. Do the person's interests, rights and reasonable expectations override it? Extra weight goes to children's data.

The interest is named in the privacy notice (Art. 13(1)(d)).

## Consent

Where consent is the basis, it is freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, by a clear affirmative act. Pre-ticked boxes and silence are not consent.

- The request is clear, in plain language and separate from other terms (Art. 7(2)).
- A service is not made conditional on consent to processing it does not need (Art. 7(4)).
- People are told before consenting that they can withdraw at any time, and withdrawing is as easy as giving consent (Art. 7(3)). Withdrawal does not affect earlier processing, but processing on that basis stops.
