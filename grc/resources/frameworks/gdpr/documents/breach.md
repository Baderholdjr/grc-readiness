## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}}, as a controller, handles personal data breaches (Art. 33, Art. 34).

## Recognising a breach

A personal data breach is a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data (Art. 4(12)). Examples: a misdirected email, a lost laptop, ransomware.

Anyone who suspects a breach reports it at once to [[TODO: the incident channel]]. {{client}} becomes aware when it is reasonably certain a breach has happened, including when a processor reports one. That time is recorded, because the 72-hour clock starts then.

## Assessing the risk

[[TODO: the privacy lead or DPO]] promptly assesses the likely risk to people: the type and sensitivity of the data, how many people, how easily they can be identified, the possible consequences, and whether the data was encrypted or recovered. The outcome is one of:

- no risk likely: recorded in the register only;
- risk: the supervisory authority is notified;
- high risk: the authority is notified and the people affected are told.

## Notifying the supervisory authority

Unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people, {{client}} notifies [[TODO: the competent or lead supervisory authority]] without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. A later notification gives the reasons for the delay. The notification contains at least (Art. 33(3)):

- the nature of the breach, including, where possible, the categories and approximate number of people and of records concerned;
- the name and contact details of the DPO or other contact point;
- the likely consequences;
- the measures taken or proposed, including to mitigate harm.

Information not yet known is given in phases without undue further delay (Art. 33(4)).

## Telling the people affected

When a breach is likely to result in a high risk to people, {{client}} tells them without undue delay, in clear and plain language, with the nature of the breach, the contact point, likely consequences and measures (Art. 34(2)). This is not needed if (Art. 34(3)):

- the data was unintelligible to anyone not authorised, such as by encryption;
- later measures mean the high risk is no longer likely;
- it would involve disproportionate effort; a public communication is used instead.

The supervisory authority can still require {{client}} to tell people (Art. 34(4)).
