## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}} transfers personal data to countries outside the European Economic Area (EEA) and to international organisations under Chapter V, so that the protection the GDPR gives is not undermined (Art. 44).

## Mapping transfers

Every transfer is recorded in the transfer map at [[TODO: location]], including onward transfers by processors and sub-processors, and remote access from outside the EEA by another entity, such as a group company, vendor or sub-processor viewing the data. Access by {{client}}'s own employees while abroad is not in itself a transfer, though the security risk still has to be managed. For each transfer the map lists the exporter, the importer and its role, the country, the data and people concerned, the purpose and the mechanism used. No new transfer starts before a mechanism is in place.

## Adequacy

Data may go to a country, territory, sector or international organisation that the European Commission has found adequate (Art. 45). For the United States, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework covers only recipients that are certified under it. {{client}} checks the recipient's certification on the official list before relying on it, and again [[TODO: frequency]]. {{client}} also checks that each adequacy decision it relies on is still in force.

## Standard contractual clauses and transfer impact assessments

Without adequacy, {{client}} uses appropriate safeguards (Art. 46), normally the Commission's 2021 standard contractual clauses with the right module:

- Module 1: controller to controller;
- Module 2: controller to processor;
- Module 3: processor to processor;
- Module 4: processor to controller.

Before relying on the clauses, {{client}} completes a transfer impact assessment. It looks at the law and practice of the destination, especially on public authority access, and decides on supplementary measures, such as encryption with keys held in the EEA, where needed. If effective protection cannot be ensured, the transfer does not go ahead or is suspended. Assessments are kept with the contract and reviewed when the law or the transfer changes.

The 2021 clauses are not designed for an importer that is itself already subject to the GDPR under Art. 3(2). For such an importer, [[TODO: the mechanism agreed with counsel]] is used, and {{client}} checks whether the Commission has adopted clauses for that case.

Binding corporate rules approved under Art. 47 may be used for transfers within {{client}}'s group. Other Art. 46 safeguards (approved codes of conduct or certification mechanisms with binding commitments by the importer, or ad hoc clauses authorised by the supervisory authority) are used only on counsel's advice.

## Derogations

The Art. 49 derogations apply only where no adequacy decision or Art. 46 safeguard is available, and are read strictly:

- explicit consent after being told of the risks (Art. 49(1)(a));
- necessity for a contract with the person, or in their interest (Art. 49(1)(b)-(c)), only for occasional transfers;
- important reasons of public interest, legal claims, vital interests, or a public register (Art. 49(1)(d)-(g)); legal claims only occasionally;
- as a last resort, compelling legitimate interests (second subparagraph of Art. 49(1)): only for a transfer that is not repetitive and concerns a limited number of people, after assessing the circumstances and providing safeguards; the supervisory authority is informed, and the person is told about the transfer and the compelling legitimate interests.

Each use is assessed and documented by [[TODO: the privacy lead]], and recorded in the records of processing (Art. 30(1)(e)). Routine or systematic transfers never rely on a derogation.
