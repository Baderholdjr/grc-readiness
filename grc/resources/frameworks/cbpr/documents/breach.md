## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}}, as a controller, tells individuals about a breach of their personal information that is likely to cause them significant harm (PR5). Security incident handling itself is in the Information Security Policy.

## Recognising a breach

A breach is any unauthorised access to, or acquisition, use, disclosure, loss or destruction of, personal information {{client}} is responsible for. Examples: an email sent to the wrong person, a lost laptop, a stolen password used to open customer records, ransomware.

Anyone who suspects a breach reports it at once to [[TODO: incident channel or contact]]. Processors and service providers report breaches to {{client}} under their contracts. The time {{client}} learned of the breach is recorded, because it starts the response.

## Assessing significant harm

[[TODO: the privacy lead]] promptly assesses whether the breach is likely to cause significant harm to the people affected. The assessment weighs:

- the type and sensitivity of the information, including sensitive and children's information;
- the number of people and how easily they can be identified;
- who obtained the information and what they could do with it;
- whether the information was encrypted, recovered or otherwise protected;
- the possible consequences: fraud, identity theft, financial loss, physical risk, discrimination, humiliation or distress.
