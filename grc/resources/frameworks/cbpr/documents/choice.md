# {{title}}

## Purpose

This procedure sets how {{client}}, as a controller, gives people choices about the collection, use and disclosure of their personal information, records those choices, honours them, and lets people withdraw consent.

## When choice is offered

Where appropriate, people can choose:

- whether their personal information is collected, with the purpose stated clearly next to the choice (PR19);
- how their information is used, with the choice offered at collection or, at the latest, before {{client}} uses it for an unrelated purpose or discloses it to a third party other than a service provider (PR20);
- whether their information is disclosed, with the choice offered at collection or, at the latest, before it is disclosed to a third party other than a service provider for an unrelated purpose (PR21).

The privacy lead decides, for each collection point, use and disclosure in the record of processing, whether choice is offered and when. Where no choice is offered, a Qualification must apply (see below).

## How choice is presented

Choice mechanisms are:

- clear and conspicuous: placed where the person will see them, not hidden behind links or pre-ticked boxes (PR23);
- clearly worded and easy to understand, in plain language that says what the person is agreeing to or refusing (PR24);
- easy to reach and free or affordable to use, through the channels the person already uses with {{client}}: [[TODO: e.g. online at collection, email, preference page, phone, post]] (PR25).

## Qualifications

Choice may not be required in limited cases. Each Qualification relied on is recorded with the collection, use or disclosure it covers and its justification, for the Accountability Agent to verify (PR19, PR20, PR21, PR26). No Qualification applies to the direct marketing opt-out (PR22):

- obviousness: the person's own action makes the purpose plain;
- publicly available information;
- technological impracticability: automatic collection when a prospective customer contacts {{client}}, with choice over use and disclosure offered afterwards;
- information received from a third party; where {{client}} engages a third party to collect for it, it instructs that party to offer choice;
- a lawful government request;
- lawful process, such as civil discovery;
- a legitimate investigation where offering choice would compromise it;
- an emergency that threatens someone's life, health or security.
